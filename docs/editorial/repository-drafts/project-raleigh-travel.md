I make websites for my adventures and use them as references during the trip. The Raleigh project applies that habit to a trip with two purposes: getting around a city and preparing for pediatric dental oral boards.

Those purposes compete for the same time. A study schedule that ignores travel is awkward to follow; a travel itinerary that ignores preparation leaves out the reason for going.

The goal is to build a plan in which study and trip logistics are easy to move between, while keeping each understandable on its own.

[Boards & beyond](https://github.com/aranlucas/raleigh-travel) groups navigation into Trip and Study. Daily itineraries link to study sessions, and session pages link back to the day and onward to the next session. Activity filters make it possible to focus on studying or exploring.

The repository also includes theme notes and recap material. The design treats a study session as something with its own page and context, rather than a long block of text buried in a calendar.

That is the useful extension of the travel-site idea: the itinerary is also an entry point into the material needed for the day. The medical study content remains reference material to check against its cited sources, not a clinical system validated by this project.

The repository contains a combined trip and preparation reference. As of this September 27 draft, the trip is still upcoming, so the result is preparation rather than a retrospective about how the exam or travel went.

Afterward, the useful follow-up is straightforward: which pages were actually opened, which study links helped, and what was too detailed to be useful on the move.
